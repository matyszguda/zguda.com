PERSONAL PERSPECTIVE / CIEPŁO

Ciepło began in a shared kitchen.

On rebuilding everyday life, Ukrainian hospitality, and an idea for making the foundation financially independent.

AS TOLD BY MATEUSZ ZGUDA

The kitchen as a meeting place

In the accommodation run by the foundation, shared kitchens quickly became an important part of everyday life. People cooked Ukrainian dishes there using ingredients we bought. They had not planned to emigrate, often did not speak the language and had not yet found something to do.

Cooking gave people something to do and brought them closer together. It helped them feel a little more at home.

An idea for what came next

We had funding for the foundation’s day-to-day work and room to think more calmly about the future. Accommodation was still needed, but we no longer had to keep increasing the number of places. We also knew that donations would not last forever.

The pandemic was coming to an end. People were returning to restaurants, and there were still vacant premises on the market. We decided to connect that opportunity with what we had seen in the shared kitchens.

Dietla 15

We found an almost ready-to-use space at 15 Dietla Street in Kraków and a supportive landlord. With enormous commitment from Olena, who had found her passion in the restaurant business, and help from many supporters, we opened a restaurant.

We called it Ciepło. It was about a sense of comfort and a place where people far from home, often against their original plans, could feel they belonged.

Food, culture, neighbourhood

We serve traditional Ukrainian food with a contemporary interpretation. The interior draws on the culture of the Carpathians, which connects parts of Poland and Ukraine. It brings to mind tradition, rest and closeness.

We display work by young Ukrainian artists on the walls. We have also organised activities to bring people together, build a community and help them get to know one another.

Relations between Poles and people who have arrived from Ukraine can be strained. We want to make our own small contribution to easing those tensions. A shared table, art and everyday hospitality are ways of doing that which feel close to us.

The business behind the mission

We financed the restaurant’s launch with a loan from the foundation. It was a risky venture. Many people who shared our mission helped get it started, and some of them were people we knew personally.

The restaurant reached break-even and built a financial buffer. At the time of writing, we are starting to repay the loan. We want Ciepło to provide lasting financial support for the foundation’s work.

As president of the management board, I am responsible for the business side of the venture. The team handles the restaurant’s day-to-day operations. There is a lot happening here, and it still gives us all energy.
